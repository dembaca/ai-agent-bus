A bus that no single chat owns.

Today Mattermost says who someone is and who belongs in which channel. The plan moves both onto the bus itself, so agents can work without any chat, and each chat (Mattermost first, later Teams or Zulip) becomes a place where people read along and answer.

From the architecture decisions on the bus without a chat as its source (ADR-016) and the operator console (ADR-015). Plans can still change.

The picture Planned

Keycloak is the root of identity. The login service lets every participant onto NATS and keeps the channel directory. Bridges, agents and the console all meet on the bus.

Identity from Keycloak Planned

A person on the bus is a Keycloak identity, keyed by its sub, not by a user name. The name stays what people read and type, but renaming someone changes neither what they own nor whom their agents trust. Chat accounts are bound to the identity, one per chat product.

  • Today’s names keep working

    An owner or trusted name resolves through its Mattermost binding. A name without a binding names nobody; it is reported, never guessed.

  • In doubt, foreign

    If a name cannot be resolved, its sender counts as another owner’s agent and is trusted by no one.

  • No chat account needed

    A person without a Mattermost account may still start agents; that chat just cannot reach them.

Source: the bus without a chat as its source (ADR-016), point 1.

A channel directory on the bus Planned

The login service keeps a directory of channels on NATS: each channel’s name on the bus, its owner, whether it is public, its members as identities, and its binding to a channel in each chat. A channel without a binding lives on NATS alone.

  • One writer

    Only the login service writes the directory. Agents cannot read or change it.

  • Who changes what

    A group of administrators creates channels and sets their owners; a channel’s owner keeps its members. First from the login service’s command line, later from the console, never by a chat command.

  • Stable names

    Renaming a channel in a chat does not change what it means on the bus.

Until the directory exists, the login service takes membership from Mattermost, behind an interface that the directory replaces. Source: ADR-016, point 2.

One bridge per chat Planned

A bridge brings what people write in their chat onto the bus and mirrors the bus into the channels bound to it. Mattermost gets the first one; Teams or Zulip would each get their own.

  • It vouches for people

    The bridge resolves a chat account to its identity and signs the event with a key the hosts pin. An account without a binding is never a trusted human.

  • Approval stays with the agent

    Whether an answer approves an action is still decided by the asking agent’s own server: its trust list, its approvers, 24 hours. Where the answer came from changes nothing.

Source: ADR-016, point 3.

Agents without a chat Planned

In a home that runs on NATS, agents talk to each other on NATS. Their chat bot is then only a mirror for people who read along, and it can go where nobody does.

Source: ADR-016, point 5.

Witnessed trust lists Planned

Today an agent’s list of trusted humans lives on its host, and other agents on NATS cannot see it. In the plan the host names its list when it connects, and the login service resolves it to identities, records it and passes it on as its word.

  • Witnessed, never set

    The list is set on the host by the agent’s owner. Nothing on the bus or in a chat sets it, and no administrator of the directory does.

  • Who sees what

    An operator sees the public channels, the channels of their own agents and those of agents that trust them directly. Never further: being trusted by someone who trusts you shows you nothing.

  • Enforced on the bus

    The rule holds in the login service, in what it lets a person read, not in the console’s screens.

Source: ADR-016, point 4.

An operator console, reading first Planned

A web console for the people who run agents, not another chat. Its only login is Keycloak, and it reads only the bus on NATS. It starts read-only: answers and approvals still come from a chat or a terminal.

  • Kanban

    One card per agent, in the column of its state.

  • Graph

    People, agents and channels, and who trusts whom.

  • Reports

    The numbers each session already reports: what agents settled without a human, and what the bus cost.

Source: the operator console (ADR-015).
